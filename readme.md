✿Autonomous Vehicle Joint Communication and Radar Technology Based on Chirped Waveforms - A Perception-Centered Paradigm ✿Abstract This paper proposes a perception-centered joint communication and millimeter wave radar paradigm to promote collaborative behavior among intelligent vehicles. Firstly, it designs a delay-Doppler orthogonal amplitude modulation (DD-QAM) based on chirped waveforms for data modulation in the delay, Doppler, and amplitude dimensions. Based on this modulation scheme, this paper derives its reachable rate to quantify communication performance. Subsequently, it introduces a dynamic environment four-dimensional (4D) parameter estimation scheme based on extended Kalman filtering, enabling active vehicles to accurately estimate the azimuth angle and tangential velocity of targets from traditional four-dimensional radar systems. Moreover, in terms of communication, this paper presents a dual-compensation demodulation and tracking scheme, allowing passive vehicles to effectively demodulate data without affecting their own perception capabilities. Simulation results validate the feasibility and superior performance of the proposed methods, providing significant progress for the development of autonomous vehicle technology.  
Introduction: The evolution of radar and communication technologies has paralleled each other over seventy years, sharing commonalities in hardware design, waveform design, and algorithmic approaches. The integration of these two fields is considered a key breakthrough direction for next-generation networks, achieving synergistic gains. The core waveforms for joint communication and radar (JCR) are primarily divided into three categories: communication center design (CCD), perception center design (SCD), and joint design (JD). Among them, while JD represents an ideal target, it faces numerous challenges due to its novelty and complexity.
Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) is a common waveform for CCD-class JCRs, capable of achieving high-speed data transmission and supporting partial perceptual capabilities. However, its peak-to-average power ratio limits it in scenarios where control data needs to be transmitted. In scenarios with high perception requirements, low communication rates, and communication being an additional feature, SCDs have greater advantages. Chirped waveforms are typical representatives of SCDs, offering advantages such as simplicity in implementation, high Doppler effect tolerance, and constant modality, suitable for pulsed and continuous wave radars. Among them, millimeter wave (mmWave) continuous wave radars, due to meeting the strict constraints of vehicle systems on size, cost, and average power, become key sensors for autonomous vehicles.
Compared to cameras and lidar, mmWave radars are cheaper and more resistant to environmental interference. However, their perception capability is limited for individual vehicles, and the efficiency of spectrum resource reuse is low, leading to interfering issues in multi-vehicle scenarios, providing opportunities for vehicle-to-vehicle collaboration and the application of JCR technology. Existing research has many shortcomings: some SCD-based JCR schemes are not suitable for civilian scenarios or have ideal assumptions and high algorithm complexity issues; LoRa technology faces challenges in extending to mmWave bands due to hardware non-idealities and excessive sampling rates; existing JCR schemes do not fully utilize time series information for data modulation of perception parameters.
To this end, this paper aims to achieve seamless integration of communication functions without compromising the basic perception capabilities of mmWave radars. The main contributions include three aspects: firstly, adopting a "listen first, speak later" mechanism to select idle frequency resources by modulating delay, Doppler, and amplitude data on a single frame chirped signal through DD-QAM, while also supporting environmental perception; secondly, leveraging relative motion during tracking to propose an extended Kalman filter (EKF)-based five-dimensional (5D) parameter estimation scheme, which adds cross-track velocity estimation compared to traditional four-dimensional perception and realizes quasi-separation estimation through spectral leakage; thirdly, based on the time series information from tracking processes, propose a dual compensation demodulation and tracking scheme that removes the modulated information while demodulating data to ensure the perception function.
Method Introduction 1. System Architecture and Frame Structure Autonomous vehicles are divided into active vehicles (AV) and passive vehicles (PV), both equipped with the same JCR transceivers, which can switch roles according to requirements. AV is responsible for sending the JCR signal that carries modulated data and receiving echoes for environmental perception, while PV is in charge of receiving signals and demodulating data to perceive the environment. Frame structure adopts Doppler Divided Multiple Access (DDM) mode assisted by beacon frames: The beacon frame only activates the first transmitting antenna, ensuring that each target has a unique detection area on the distance-Doppler map (RDM), achieving unambiguous estimation of distance and velocity. DDM frames activate all transmit antennas, with each target corresponding to multiple detection areas for distinguishing the transmit antennas and estimating the azimuth and elevation angles.
2. The core modulation and signal model proposed for DD-QAM modulation scheme directly modulates data onto the delay, Doppler, and complex amplitude of chirped signals. Under a single-input-single-output (SISO) channel, the mathematical model of the received signal is derived through signal mixing, low-pass filtering, and sampling processes; under a multipath multiinput multioutput (MIMO) channel, the DDM scheme is employed to achieve waveform orthogonality, enhance angle resolution through antenna aperture expansion, and derive the AV and PV received signal models.
3. The parameter estimation scheme design includes a 4D parameter estimation scheme assisted by beacon frames, utilizing CFAR detection to extract the delay and Doppler information of targets (paths) from RDM. It enhances estimation accuracy through the average improvement in detection positions due to spectrum leakage, followed by DFT-based algorithms for estimating azimuth and elevation angles. This scheme addresses the issue of reduced maximum detection speed in the DDM scheme by using beacon frames, and can complete parameter estimation solely with DDM frames after initial perception phase.
4. Tracking and demodulation schemes
Active Vehicle: Proposes a 5D parameter estimation scheme based on EKF, integrating traditional 4D parameters (distance, radial velocity, azimuth, elevation) with tangential velocity. By fusing historical prior information with current observation values, the accuracy of parameter estimation is enhanced and the target azimuth angle is obtained.
✧  
Passive Vehicle: Proposes a dual compensation demodulation and tracking scheme. It first predicts the parameters of the current AV based on historical parameters, then compares the predicted values with the current observed values to demodulate delay, Doppler, and amplitude dimensions data, finally removes the modulation information and uses EKF to complete the update of AV parameters for tracking, achieving synergy between perception and communication.
Conclusion This paper focuses on enhancing the communication capabilities of centimeter-wave frequency perception centered autonomous vehicles. The main contributions are threefold: Firstly, in terms of waveform design, a chirp waveform based on DD-QAM is proposed to achieve efficient data modulation across delay, Doppler, and complex amplitude dimensions; Secondly, in terms of perception, a 4D parameter estimation scheme assisted by beacon frames is introduced to perform quasi-separation estimations for delays and Dopplers, while an EKF-based 5D parameter estimation scheme supports dynamic separation estimations and can additionally acquire tangential speed and azimuth angle information; Thirdly, in terms of communication, a dual compensation demodulation and tracking scheme for passive vehicles is designed, utilizing time series information to complete data demodulation without affecting perception functions.
Although this design is centered on perception, it provides a new path for enhancing communication rates. Future research can explore the direction of extending bit rate based on the number of chirps per frame, further optimizing the comprehensive performance of joint communication and radar systems.
